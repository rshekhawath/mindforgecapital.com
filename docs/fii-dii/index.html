FII / DII activity — common questions

What is FII / DII activity?

FII stands for Foreign Institutional Investors and DII stands for Domestic Institutional Investors — the large professional investors whose buying and selling moves Indian markets. FII/DII activity is the net value each group bought or sold in the cash market on a given day. A net-positive figure means the group was a net buyer; a net-negative figure means it was a net seller.

What does the FII index-futures long/short ratio mean?

It is the number of long (buy) index-futures contracts held by FIIs divided by the number of short (sell) contracts. A ratio above 1 means FIIs hold more longs than shorts and are positioned net long; a ratio below 1 means they hold more shorts and are positioned net short. It is a quick read of how foreign institutions are positioned in index derivatives.

Where does this FII / DII data come from?

The figures are provisional disclosures sourced from the NSE — the cash-market provisional numbers and the participant-wise F&O reports. Index levels come from public market data. Provisional cash numbers can be revised later by the exchange.

How often is the FII / DII data updated?

It is refreshed every market day, after the exchange publishes its end-of-day provisional figures. Weekends and market holidays show the last available trading day. This is a research dashboard, not investment advice.
