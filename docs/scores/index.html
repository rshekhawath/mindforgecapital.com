Integrity Score — common questions

What is the Integrity Score?

The Integrity Score is a free 0-100 grade for every NSE-listed company, built from two pillars: Quality (profitability and balance-sheet health) and Value (how cheap the stock is versus its fundamentals). It is a systematic read-out of a company's fundamentals, not a buy or sell recommendation.

What is a good Integrity Score?

Higher is better. As a rough guide, 80 and above is excellent, 65 to 79 is good, 50 to 64 is fair, 35 to 49 is weak and below 35 is poor. The score is a starting point for research — always read the full scorecard and do your own due diligence before making any decision.

How are Quality and Value scored?

Quality is measured from metrics like return on equity, return on capital, margins, debt levels and earnings stability. Value is measured from valuation ratios such as price-to-earnings, price-to-book and free-cash-flow yield. Each company is scored against the whole NSE universe, and the two pillars combine into the overall 0-100 Integrity Score.

Is the Integrity Score free?

Yes. The Integrity Score and the full scorecard for every NSE-listed company are free to use, with no sign-up required. Data is refreshed daily from public filings.

How the score works

Each stock is ranked against the entire NSE universe on a set of factors. For every factor a stock sits somewhere from the bottom (0) to the top (100) of all companies — those percentile ranks are averaged into lens scores. The Overall Integrity Score is the average of Quality & Value; Growth, Momentum and Efficiency are shown alongside as separate lenses.
