Reels make it sound like you must learn the market, trade options, or settle for whatever a fund gives you. There are more choices than that, and each one costs something. This page sets them out in plain words, says who MindForge does not suit, and shows what you would do first.

A fund is not a bad choice. If you want no orders at all and the lowest tax, a low-cost fund may be the better one. What a model portfolio offers instead is a short list built to try to beat its index. It may not, and it can fall harder on the way.

And the FD? It is the right home for money with a date on it. Its interest is taxed at your slab each year, so at the 30% slab a 7% FD keeps about 4.8%. An example rate, not a quote.

What is a normal year? Across every five-year stretch since 2007, the Nifty 50’s price index returned a middle figure of 11.7% a year, from −1.0% to 22.3%, before dividends. Doing much better takes more concentration, and that brings deeper falls. What reels leave out

Is this for you?

It may suit you if

  • The money can stay invested for at least five years.
  • Your salary covers your life, and six months of expenses are set aside somewhere safe.
  • You could watch it fall by a quarter or more without selling.
  • You can spare about ten minutes a month, and one extra form at tax time.

It will not suit you if

  • You are carrying a credit-card balance or a costly personal loan. Clearing that is a return nothing here can promise.
  • You need this money for a home, a wedding or fees in the next few years.
  • You want it to double quickly.
  • You would rather place no orders at all. A fund does that for you.
  • Your employer bars selling within six months of buying.

All at once, or in parts?

In Indian index data, putting it all in at once ended ahead of six monthly parts a little more often than not after five years. When the market fell soon after the start, going in parts usually did better. So this is a choice about regret as much as return, and it is yours. The numbers

On MindForge, in parts means entering only this month’s part as your amount, then using Add money on later 1sts. Each part has to clear the portfolio’s floor, or the priciest names wait.

From tonight to your first orders

  1. Tonight, five minutes. Sign up. The free portfolio’s link arrives by e-mail in minutes. A paid plan’s arrives within 24 hours, with payment set up on WhatsApp, and its first month is free.
  2. No demat yet? A day or two. Open one online with your PAN, an Aadhaar-linked mobile and your bank account. How to open one
  3. Before you place orders. Move the money to your broker. A buy is checked against your cash when you place it. How to add funds
  4. The evening you start. Enter your amount and place the orders, then tick each one. Month one is buys only. On Zerodha the orders go as baskets. On any other broker it is one order at a time, down a numbered list: allow about twenty minutes the first month. What you will see
  5. Then nothing until the 1st. It will go up and down. The dashboard shows what is normal.
  6. Next month. Usually two short sittings: the sales one evening, the buys the next. Your demat has to approve the sales.
  7. At tax time. ITR-2 instead of ITR-1, and your broker’s report does most of it. What changes
