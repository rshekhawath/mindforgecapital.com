Before your first order

Opening a demat, and what comes next

A demat account is where your shares live. Opening one takes a few minutes of your time and a day or two of your broker’s. Here is what you need, what you can skip, and how the money gets there.

In short

  • You need three things: your PAN, a mobile number linked to Aadhaar, and a bank account in your name.
  • You do not need F&O switched on. Brokers ask for income proof only for that, and you can skip it.
  • The money comes next. A buy is checked against the cash in your trading account when you place it, so move the money before the evening you place orders.

What you are opening

Two accounts, usually opened together. A demat account holds your shares. A trading account places your orders. When people say “open a demat”, they mean both.

What you need, and how long it takes

  • Your PAN.
  • An Aadhaar number linked to your mobile, for the OTPs.
  • A bank account in your name. A cancelled cheque or a statement helps if the automatic check fails.
  • A phone or laptop with a camera, for a short video check and an e-sign.

Your name has to match on PAN, Aadhaar and the e-sign, or the forms come back. Zerodha, for example, says an account opens within 48 working hours, and you can place orders only once the exchanges have activated it. So do not leave it for the evening you want to invest.

Which broker

Any Indian broker holds your shares the same way: in a demat account in your name. What differs is what it charges and how you place orders. If you follow a list of many orders a month, check whether your broker can take a group of orders at once. Zerodha calls this a basket, and it is what the MindForge dashboard fills in; on other brokers the dashboard gives you a numbered list instead. This page carries no referral link.

What to skip

  • F&O. The form may ask whether you want futures and options. You do not need them to buy shares. SEBI’s study of FY26 found that 87.7% of individual traders in them lost money.
  • Margin. Buying with borrowed money is how you can lose more than you put in.
  • MIS on the order screen. That is intraday: the broker closes it the same day. Shares you mean to keep are delivery, shown as CNC.

Moving the money

A buy is checked against the cash in your trading account when you place it, so the money has to be there first. On Zerodha you start from Kite → Funds → Add funds, from the bank account linked to your trading account. Zerodha’s pages set out three routes:

UPIFree and instant. Up to ₹5 lakh a transfer, or less if your bank says so. Start it from Add funds, not from a UPI app.
Net bankingInstant, for ₹9 plus GST a transfer. Zerodha sets no cap; your bank may.
NEFT or RTGSFree at Zerodha, though your bank may charge. No limit from Zerodha, so this is the usual way for a large amount. It can take a couple of hours.

A large amount is worth moving a day early. Your bank’s own daily limit may be lower than you expect, and it is easier to find that out before the evening you planned to invest. Other brokers work the same way, with their own limits.

What can go wrong on the first evening

“Insufficient funds”The money is not in your trading account yet, or falls short. Prices move, so leave a little spare.
Account not activeThe exchanges have not activated it yet. Wait for your broker’s e-mail.
Market closedThat is fine. Orders placed after hours go as after-market orders (AMO), and reach the exchange at the next morning’s open.
It asks for a TPINOnly a sale needs your demat’s approval. A buy does not. How selling works

Broker details read from Zerodha’s support pages on 5 November 2026. They change, and your broker’s own page is the final word. General information, not advice.
