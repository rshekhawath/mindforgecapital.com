SWP calculator

How long does a corpus actually last?

A systematic withdrawal plan takes a fixed amount out every month while the rest stays invested. The calculator shows what is left at the end of your horizon, what you will have drawn in total, and — if the withdrawal is too large for the corpus — the month it runs out.

How an SWP is modelled here

Month by month: the balance grows by one twelfth of the annual return, then the withdrawal is taken out. If a month's balance cannot cover the withdrawal, the remainder is paid and the plan ends there — the calculator reports that month rather than showing a negative balance.

balance = balance × (1 + i) − withdrawal, each month

The withdrawal rate is the number to watch

Annual withdrawal divided by the corpus is the figure that decides everything. Draw meaningfully less than the return you earn and the corpus keeps growing while paying you; draw more and it shrinks — slowly at first, then quickly, because every rupee withdrawn also stops earning.

What this model does not capture

  • Sequence risk. A constant return is the friendliest possible assumption for a withdrawal plan. Two portfolios with the same average return can end very differently if one of them falls early, because withdrawals during a drawdown sell more units at lower prices.
  • Inflation in the withdrawal. The amount you take out here stays the same every month. In practice the cost of living rises, so a plan that just works today is usually short later.
  • Tax. Each withdrawal from a mutual fund is a redemption, and the gain portion is taxable. The figures here are gross.

Questions

What is a safe monthly withdrawal?
There is no universal number, and any calculator that offers one is overstating what it knows. As a sanity check, an annual withdrawal well below the return you can defend leaves the corpus intact; the closer the two get, the more the outcome depends on the order in which returns arrive.
Does the calculator increase my withdrawal with inflation?
No — the withdrawal is constant. To approximate a rising withdrawal, run the calculator at a higher amount, or use a lower net return to stand in for the erosion.
When is the withdrawal taken?
At the end of each month, after that month's growth. Taking it at the start would exhaust the corpus slightly faster.
Is an SWP better than a dividend or an interest payout?
It is more predictable: you choose the amount, rather than taking whatever a fund or a bank declares. The tax treatment differs by instrument, and only the gain portion of each withdrawal is taxed in a mutual fund SWP.
