How an EMI is calculated

An EMI is the fixed monthly payment that clears a reducing-balance loan exactly at the end of its tenure. Each payment is split: interest on the outstanding balance first, and whatever is left reduces the principal.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [ (1 + r)n − 1 ]

where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual ÷ 12) and n is the number of months.

Why the early years feel like nothing is happening

Because they nearly aren't. On a 20-year home loan at 8.5%, about four-fifths of the first year's payments are interest and only a fifth touches the principal. The chart on this page shows that split year by year — the blue block is principal, the amber block is interest, and they swap places as the loan matures.

Three things that change the answer more than you expect

  • Tenure. Stretching a home loan from 15 to 25 years cuts the EMI by roughly a fifth and can add more than half the loan amount again in interest.
  • Rate. On a floating-rate home loan a single percentage point over twenty years is usually worth several lakh rupees.
  • Prepayment. Money paid early comes straight off the principal, so it saves every future interest charge that principal would have attracted.

Assumptions this makes

  • A fixed rate for the whole tenure, equal monthly instalments, and no prepayments, moratorium or part-disbursement.
  • No processing fee, insurance, GST or documentation charge — those are paid on top of the figures here.
  • A floating-rate loan re-prices when the benchmark moves; when it does, banks usually change the tenure rather than the EMI.

Questions

Is a shorter tenure always better?
It always costs less in total interest, but only if the higher EMI is comfortable. A tenure you can service through a bad year is worth more than an interest saving you have to refinance out of.
How much interest do I actually pay?
The calculator reports it directly: total interest is the sum of every instalment minus the amount you borrowed. On a long home loan it is frequently of the same order as the loan itself.
Should I prepay the loan or invest the money?
Prepaying earns you a guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan rate. Investing has to beat that rate after tax, with risk, to be the better choice. Compare the loan rate here against what you would honestly assume in the SIP calculator.
Does this work for a personal or car loan?
Yes — switch the loan type at the top. The arithmetic is identical; only the typical amount, rate and tenure differ, and those defaults change with the selection.
Why is my bank's EMI slightly different?
Rounding, the day your instalment falls relative to disbursement, and any fee the bank finances into the loan. The difference is usually small; the structure is the same.
