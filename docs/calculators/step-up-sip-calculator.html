Step-up SIP · inflation-adjusted

A SIP that grows with your income

Most people can invest more next year than this year. A step-up SIP raises the instalment by a fixed percentage every twelve months — and this calculator also reports what the final corpus is worth in today's rupees, because a number thirty years out is easy to misread.

Result

Raising the SIP is one lever. The return is the other.

Over twenty years a 10% annual step-up roughly doubles a flat SIP’s corpus — largely because you put in nearly three times as much — while two extra points of return add about a third on the same contributions. How a step-up SIP is calculated

There is no single closed-form line for this, because the instalment changes. The calculator runs the schedule month by month: each month's instalment is added to the balance, the balance grows at one twelfth of the annual return, and every twelfth month the instalment is increased by the step-up percentage.

balance = (balance + instalment) × (1 + i)  ·  instalment × (1 + step) every 12 months

Why the inflation-adjusted figure is the one to read

A thirty-year projection in future rupees flatters itself. At 6% inflation, prices roughly double every twelve years — so a corpus of ₹5 crore in 2056 buys what a little under ₹90 lakh buys today. The calculator shows both figures side by side, and the second one is the one to plan against.

What a step-up actually buys you

  • It front-loads less and back-loads more. The later instalments are larger but compound for less time, so a step-up adds less than the same money invested earlier would have.
  • It tracks your salary. A 10% step-up on a 10% raise keeps your savings rate constant instead of letting lifestyle absorb the increase.
  • It is not a substitute for the return. Compare a 10% step-up against a two-point higher return in this calculator; over twenty years the step-up moves the corpus about three times as far, but only because it also asks for far more money — the return is the lever that works on what you already invest.

Assumptions this makes

  • Instalments at the start of each month; the step-up applies once every twelve months, including in the final year.
  • A constant return, compounded monthly, and a constant inflation rate for the real-value figure.
  • No fees or taxes. Markets do not deliver the same return every year, so treat the output as an illustration of compounding — not a forecast, and not investment advice.

Questions

What step-up percentage should I use?
Something you can actually sustain — most people set it near their expected annual salary increase, commonly 5% to 10%. A step-up you abandon in year three is worth less than a flat SIP you keep.
Is the step-up applied every year including the last?
Yes. The instalment rises at the start of each new twelve-month block, so a 15-year plan at 10% ends with an instalment about 3.8 times the first one.
What does the inflation-adjusted value mean?
It is the maturity corpus divided by (1 + inflation) raised to the number of years — the same buying power expressed in today's rupees. Use it when the goal is a real-world cost like a house or an education.
Does a step-up SIP beat a flat SIP of the same total?
No. If the total amount invested is identical, the flat SIP wins, because its money goes in earlier and compounds for longer. A step-up wins when it lets you invest more overall.
