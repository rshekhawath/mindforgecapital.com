How the date is worked out

The calculator steps forward a month at a time. Each month your monthly amount goes in at the start, the whole balance grows at the return you assume (less any yearly cost), and the goal grows with inflation. The first month the balance covers the goal is the answer.

balance = (balance + monthly) × (1 + (return − cost) ÷ 12), each month  ·  goalm = goal × (1 + inflation)m/12

Reading the result

  • Time to goal — years and months until your money first covers the goal, in that month’s money.
  • The goal then — what it costs on that day, after inflation.
  • You put in — your lump sum plus every monthly amount until then; Growth is the rest.
  • A yearly cost adds — how much later the same goal arrives when that cost comes off the return every year.

Assumptions this makes

  • A constant return, compounded monthly; the monthly amount goes in at the start of each month and stays the same.
  • The goal grows at a constant inflation rate.
  • No taxes. The yearly cost comes off the return each month, the way a fund’s expense ratio comes out of its value. Markets do not deliver the same return every year, so treat the date as an illustration of compounding — not a forecast, and not investment advice.

Questions

How is the date worked out?
Month by month. Your lump sum and each monthly amount grow at the return you set, less any yearly cost, the goal grows with inflation, and the date is the first month your money covers the goal.
Why does the goal grow?
Because prices do. A goal that costs ₹1 crore today costs about ₹1.8 crore in ten years at 6% inflation, and a plan that aims at the old figure arrives short.
What does a yearly cost do to the date?
It pushes it back. A cost taken every year compounds against you the way the return compounds for you, so even 1% a year can move a long goal by a year or more. Set the cost above and the result shows how much later. The Fee Calculator compares a flat fee with a percentage on your own amount.
Is the return guaranteed?
No. The calculator assumes the same return every year, and markets never deliver that. A real path is bumpier, and a fall near the goal date can push it back. Treat the date as a guide to how the numbers relate, not a promise.
